# Advisor Inquiry and Application Email

Use this template when the user requests an advisor outreach email. The complete email, including salutation and sign-off but excluding the subject line and source notes, must contain 150-200 words.

## Subject

Use a direct subject such as `Prospective PhD Student Inquiry – [Research Area]` or `Application for [Exact Position Title]`. Do not imply an application to a specific position unless the user supplied or confirmed that position.

## Email

Dear Professor [Surname],

My name is [Full Name], and I am [current degree/program or concise identity]. I am writing to inquire whether you are considering new [degree-level] students for [term/year] / I am writing to apply for the [exact position title] at [university/institution].

I am currently studying [major/field] at [university]. My research interests include [specific interests]. My [thesis/dissertation/project] examines [topic] under the supervision of [name], if applicable. Through [relevant research experience, methods, or publication], I have developed experience in [relevant capability]. Your research on [verified topic keywords] particularly interests me, and my background in [user's experience or interest] overlaps with your work on [specific verified topic].

I have attached my CV for your consideration. Thank you for your time. I would welcome the opportunity to discuss whether my interests and background could fit your group / the requirements of this position.

Best regards,
[Full Name]

## Drafting rules

- Keep the first paragraph to exactly two sentences: identity and contact purpose.
- Keep the background paragraph to approximately four or five sentences. Include only details supported by the user's CV or description.
- Use a concise closing that thanks the professor and proposes a relevant next step.
- Keep the email between 150 and 200 words. Report the count and state whether it includes the salutation and sign-off; by default, include both and exclude the subject and source list.
- Include the CV attachment sentence only if the user confirms that the CV will be attached. Otherwise write: `I would be happy to share my CV and any additional materials.`
- For a student inquiry, ask whether the professor is considering students; do not call it an application to a position. For a position-based application, use the exact title and institution supplied or verified.
- Do not claim to have read or used a named paper unless the user confirms it. Do not include a paper title merely as proof of personalized outreach. Research alignment may be described using verified topic keywords.
- Put the official profile and verified publication URLs in source notes after the email, not inside the email body, unless the user asks to cite them in the email.
- Never invent the professor's title, institution, openings, the user's credentials, a thesis supervisor, or an attachment.
